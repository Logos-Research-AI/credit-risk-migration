### C-g. `PyLinks` 6, 8 and 12: the fit, the read-backs, the sum

code outside the proofs go into `PyLinks` as one correction. Decision (2): the port follows SAS on
a training part with one outcome. Decision (3): G2 covers train steps 03 and 05 to 08.

Vocabulary. For a table T, *the outcomes of T* are the `default_flag` values of its complete
cases, as step 01 of train computes them (`y_train`: the rows where the 36 candidates and
`default_flag` are all present). T is *a training frame* when, as a frame, its column names are
distinct, its columns have one length, and the 36 candidates and `default_flag` are numeric
columns (the three table conditions of `train_run_01_fit_frame_precond`). T *has both outcomes*
when every outcome is 0 or 1 and both 0 and 1 occur. T *has one outcome* when no two outcomes
differ (this includes no outcome at all).

## 7. Risks and open points

**R1. Steps 07 and 08 ask a bound on the number of rows that valid input does not give.**
`hosmer_lemeshow_precond` asks `yy.length ≤ 4503599627370496` (2^52) and
`classification_table_precond` asks `y.length ≤ 2251799813685248` (2^51), both "narrowing"
conditions for integers a float holds exactly (RULEBOOK of the port, section 6); Python raises
on neither. `InputOK` has no row bound (ruling G56: "`InputOK` gets no row bound"). So G2 with
steps 07 and 08 is false at a valid training table with enough rows, for the T1 parameters; a
negation could be proved. No clause of `PyLinks` can supply the bound truthfully: it is not a
fact about out-of-scope code, and clause 3 already says the importer returns on every valid
table. Ways out, all outside this draft: (a) the owner adds a row bound to `TrainOK` (the limit
of G56, fewer than 10^8 rows, made formal, would do; G56 chose not to); (b) the port translators
drop the two bounds from the preconditions where the number type is exact (the units are generic
in the number type, so this is a change of the units, not of the proofs); (c) G2 keeps steps 07
and 08 out, against decision (3). Until one is taken, G2 as drafted in section 4 must not be
sent to a prover as a statement to prove.

**R2. Decision (2) is not confined to F4.** After the port change the real train stage writes no
`model.json` and no coefficient file on a one-outcome training part, scores every training row
with a missing probability, and writes the validation score file with a header only
(`train.py:292-300`, `:489-492`, `:566-569` of the working tree; x27). The SAS Lean still takes
the fit as a total parameter: `sasPipeline.fit` returns `some (sas.fit ...)` whenever the two
flags hold, and `score` scores every row with that model. On valid input with a one-outcome
training part:
- E3 (`P.fit F1 = S.fit F1`) and A2 ("the model the train stage returns is `P.fit`") cannot both
  survive: either `pyPipeline.fit` returns nothing there (E3 false against the SAS `some`), or it
  keeps returning a model the stage does not return (A2 false);
- E5 and F3 compare the port's training scores (probability missing) with tables computed from a
  model (probability present on the SAS Lean side for F3);
- E8 to E11, F5, F6, G4 hang on `S.score m F1 = some S1` and on the port's metrics stage reading
  a header-only validation file.
F4 escapes only because the SAS `model_summary` loses its row through the one-class ROC whatever
the model is. So "no contract keeps a hypothesis of its own" and "the port follows SAS on one

**R3. 6b (4), "no leverage equals 1", is an idealisation.** In exact arithmetic a leverage equals
1 exactly when one complete case alone carries a direction of the final model (for instance a
flag that is 1 on one training row and is in the final model). That is a quasi-separated fit;
the port keeps SAS's estimates there and usually removes the variable at the next Wald test, but
I found nothing that excludes such a final model. Python does not raise there (`1.0 - h` equal
to 0 gives an infinite value and a probability of 0 or 1; `sas_compat.py:1553`); the Lean
precondition excludes it as a "narrowing". So the clause is true of the port in the sense
"the port never stops there" and assumed in the sense the Lean states. Same class as ruling G56.
Alternative: the port translators remove the narrowing from `classification_table_precond`.

**R4. 6b (3), a positive variance for every parameter of the final model, is assumed on one
path.** A parameter left out of a singular information matrix has variance exactly 0
(`sas_compat.py:801-817`), and on such a final model the real port raises `ZeroDivisionError` at
`train.py:374` (`(b / se) ** 2`). Stepwise selection removes a left-out variable at the next

model it had before; I could not show from the code that this model never has a left-out
parameter. Not observed in any recorded run. If it can happen it is a defect of the port (or an
unmeasured SAS behaviour), and 6b (3) is false there. Proposed: accept as an assumption under
G58 with this note, and ask the coding side for a test or an argument.
